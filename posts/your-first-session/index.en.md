Most people who book their first session at HAKU tell us the same thing when they arrive: *“I’m not really sure what this is.”* That is completely fine. You do not need to believe in anything, know any terminology, or prepare in a special way. This post walks through a typical first visit, so the only surprise is how quiet the hour feels.

## Before you arrive

When you book, we ask for your name, a contact number and which session you would like. If you are unsure, choose **Sound & breath** (45 minutes). It is the gentlest introduction and gives you a feel for the room without committing to a longer session.

There is nothing to bring. Wear clothes you can lie down in comfortably — soft trousers and a top without a stiff collar are ideal. You keep your clothes on for every session. If you wear contact lenses and tend to doze off, you may want glasses that day.

Try to arrive five minutes early. The walk from Kamakura Station takes about eight minutes, and arriving unhurried makes a noticeable difference to how quickly you settle.

## Tea and a conversation

Every session begins at a small table by the window. We make a pot of tea — usually a light roasted hojicha — and talk for ten to fifteen minutes.

This is not an interview. We simply ask how you are feeling today, whether anything is on your mind, and what you are hoping to take away from the time. Some guests want to rest after a demanding month. Others are curious, or have been recommended by a friend. Any answer is a good answer, including “I don’t know.”

It is also the moment to mention anything that would make you more comfortable: a sensitive shoulder, a dislike of strong scents, a preference for silence or for being told what is happening. We adjust the session around you.

## Lying down

The treatment room is warm and dimly lit, with a wide, padded table, a soft blanket and a pillow for under your knees. You lie on your back, fully clothed, and we check that you are warm enough.

During Reiki, the practitioner rests their hands lightly on or just above areas such as the shoulders, the head and the feet, staying in each position for a few minutes. There is no pressure and no massage. In a crystal light session, a few crystals are also placed on the blanket around you; in a sound session, singing bowls are played near you at a low volume.

Your only job is to rest. You can keep your eyes open or closed. You do not need to concentrate, visualise or “do it right”.

## What people notice

Every guest is different, so we never promise a particular experience. Commonly, people describe:

- a sense of warmth where the hands are resting
- a heaviness in the arms and legs, as before sleep
- their thoughts slowing down, or simply drifting
- falling asleep altogether — which is welcome

Some people notice very little in the moment and only realise afterwards that they feel calmer. That counts too. Energy healing at HAKU is a relaxation practice; it is not a medical treatment and is not a substitute for care from your doctor.

## Coming back

At the end, we let you know quietly that the session is finishing and give you a few minutes to stretch before sitting up. Moving slowly is a good idea — many guests feel pleasantly heavy.

Back at the table there is water and a little more time to talk, if you want it. You can share anything you noticed, ask questions, or just finish your water in silence. There is no pressure to book again.

## After your visit

On the day itself, we suggest drinking plenty of water, eating something light, and leaving a little space in your evening if you can. A short walk by the sea is a lovely way to end the afternoon.

If you would like to come back, most guests find that a session every three or four weeks fits comfortably into ordinary life. But the best rhythm is simply the one that feels right to you.

> Rest is not a reward. It is where you begin.

We look forward to welcoming you.
